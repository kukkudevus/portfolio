01 · The problem
Many traders don’t lose money because they lack knowledge. They lose it through behavior.

Chasing losses right after a bad trade, moving a stop-loss further away, or trading on after a bad session. Advice like “trade with discipline” is easy to agree with and hard to practice.

02 · What I did

Turned advice into rules a product can act on

I defined trading-discipline rules, each with a clear trigger and a reason it helps:

Common adviceThe ruleWhy it helps
“Know when to stop”A loss limit for each practice sessionPuts a hard ceiling on a bad day
“Always use a stop-loss”A stop-loss must be in place as soon as a trade is openedNo trade runs without defined risk
“Don’t revenge trade”A short cooldown after a losing tradeStops emotional, impulsive re-entry

03 · Learning design

Drawing on learning research (cognitive load, deliberate practice, and spaced repetition), I recommended teaching one rule per session, designing scenarios that tempt the user to break it, varying the market context so the habit carries over, and combining rules only after each one is stable.

04 · Product direction

I studied how Duolingo, HackerRank, and StockGro attract and keep learners, and proposed practical methods to test whether traders would use a standalone practice product.

05 · What came of it

These rules shaped the product’s practice plan. Before practicing, users get a suggested set of rules, such as a daily loss limit and a stop-loss set before every trade, which they can edit or remove, as long as at least one rule stays.

Learn by Doing was later combined with FYERS Academy: Academy teaches through video lessons, quizzes, and certification, and Learn by Doing lets people practice what they learned.
